If you sell in more than one country, Cash on Delivery rarely costs the same everywhere. One courier charges a collection fee and another doesn't. In one market COD is what most customers expect, and in another hardly anyone uses it. A single fee for every country will be wrong somewhere.

The better approach is to set the fee market by market. Here is how to work out what each country needs and set it up on Shopify.

## Work out what each market needs

Before creating any rules, look at each country you sell to and answer three questions:

- **Does your courier charge for cash collection there?** This is the most direct reason to add a fee.
- **How often are COD orders refused?** Compare refused parcels with delivered ones. A market with many refusals has a stronger case for a fee.
- **Do customers have another way to pay?** Where card or wallet payments are common, a fee nudges shoppers towards them. Where COD is the main way people pay, a high fee may simply lose you the sale.

You'll usually end up with three groups: countries that need a fee, countries that need a smaller fee, and countries that need none.

## How to set it up with ChargeIt

[ChargeIt](/products/chargeit) can limit any rule by country, so a fee only applies where you want it.

1. **Install ChargeIt** (free) and open it from your Shopify admin.
2. **Turn on the payment method selector** for your cart page.
3. **Create a rule** and choose Cash on Delivery as the payment method.
4. **Set the fee** as a fixed amount or a percentage, and name the fee line.
5. **Limit the rule by country,** choosing only the markets it should apply to.
6. **Save.** Shoppers in other countries won't be charged this fee.

To charge a different fee in another group of countries, create a separate rule for that group and repeat the steps. Keep each country in one rule only, so you always know which fee applies.

## Fixed amount or percentage?

If you sell in one currency, either works. If you sell in several, a percentage is often the simpler choice, because it scales with the order in any currency. If you prefer a fixed amount, test it in each market before going live, so you can see exactly what customers there will be charged.

Whichever you choose, place a test order for each country and confirm the fee line appears only where it should.

## When a fee isn't the right answer

Sometimes the problem in a market is too big for a fee to solve. If COD refusals there are costing you more than a fee could recover, you have two other options:
