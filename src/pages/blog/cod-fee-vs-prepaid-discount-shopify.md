There are two common ways to reduce the cost of Cash on Delivery on a Shopify store. You can charge a fee on COD orders, or give a discount on prepaid ones. Both make paying upfront the better deal, but they feel very different to a customer and they affect your margin in different ways.

Here is how to decide which one fits your store.

## The difference in one line

- **A COD fee** recovers money from the orders that cost you more.
- **A prepaid discount** gives money away on the orders that cost you less.

Customers generally respond better to a reward than to a charge, but the reward costs you something on every prepaid order, including orders from people who would have paid online anyway.

## When a COD fee makes more sense

A fee tends to be the better fit when:

- **Your courier charges for cash collection,** so the fee simply passes on a real cost.
- **Your margins are thin** and you can't afford to discount.
- **Most of your orders are already prepaid,** so a discount would mostly go to customers who didn't need persuading.
- **Refused parcels are costly,** and you want COD orders to carry some of that risk.

The main risk is losing the sale. If COD is the only way some customers can pay, a fee is a cost they can't avoid, so keep it modest.

## When a prepaid discount makes more sense

A discount tends to be the better fit when:

- **Most of your orders are COD,** so each customer who switches saves you a real cost.
- **Your market is competitive** and a fee could send shoppers to a store that doesn't charge one.
- **You have room in your margin,** and the discount costs less than the refusals it prevents.
- **You want to promote it,** because "Save 5% when you pay by card" works as a marketing message and "COD costs extra" doesn't.

The main risk is paying for behaviour you already had. Work out the cost across all your prepaid orders, not just the customers who switch.

## How to compare them with your own numbers

You can get a useful answer with a few figures from your order history:

1. **Count your orders** by payment method over the last few months.
2. **Work out what COD costs you** per order, including collection fees and the cost of refused parcels spread across all COD orders.
3. **Estimate the cost of a discount:** the discount amount multiplied by every prepaid order you expect, including the ones you already get.
4. **Compare the two.** If the discount costs more than the COD losses it could prevent, start with a fee.

These are estimates, so treat the result as a starting point. The most reliable answer comes from trying one change at a time and watching your COD share, refused parcels and sales.

## Why many stores use both

A fee and a discount aren't mutually exclusive. Used together, even small amounts create a clear gap between the two ways of paying. A store could keep the fee low enough not to scare anyone off and the discount low enough to protect its margin, while the combined difference is still easy to see.
